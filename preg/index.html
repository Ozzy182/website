First Trimester

Your wife is currently in this stage. Hormonal and physical changes can begin quickly, even before there is a visible baby bump.

Common Early-Pregnancy Changes

These are commonly experienced and are often not an emergency by themselves. Symptoms can be mild, strong, come and go, or be absent entirely.

😴 Tiredness

She may need more sleep, feel drained during the day or become tired after normal activities.

🤢 Nausea or Vomiting

“Morning sickness” may happen at any time of day or night.

💗 Tender Breasts

Breasts may feel sore, swollen, heavy or more sensitive than usual.

🚻 Frequent Urination

Needing to urinate more often, including at night, is common.

🎭 Mood Changes

Hormonal changes and the adjustment to pregnancy may cause emotional ups and downs.

👃 Smell and Food Changes

Smells may seem stronger, and familiar foods may suddenly be appealing or unpleasant.

🎈 Bloating or Constipation

Digestion may slow down, causing gas, bloating, constipation or indigestion.

🩹 Mild Cramping

Mild period-like cramps can occur. Severe, persistent or one-sided pain needs medical advice.

💧 More Discharge

Clear or milky-white discharge can increase. A bad smell, itching or pain should be checked.

✨ Few Symptoms

Some people have very few noticeable symptoms. Symptom intensity alone cannot confirm how a pregnancy is progressing.

What may help you both stay calm

Symptoms often change from day to day. A quieter symptom day does not automatically mean something is wrong. At the same time, never ignore severe, persistent or worrying symptoms.

Spotting or bleeding

Light spotting can happen in early pregnancy, but any bleeding during pregnancy should be reported to her obstetrician, midwife or other healthcare provider for individual advice.

First-Trimester Priorities

💙 How the partner can help now

  • Take tiredness and nausea seriously, even when she does not look visibly pregnant.
  • Help with meals, smells or household tasks that trigger nausea.
  • Attend appointments when possible and keep a shared list of questions.
  • Offer reassurance without dismissing symptoms that worry her.
🌼

Second Trimester

The baby bump becomes more noticeable, and some people find that nausea and tiredness improve during this stage.

Common Second-Trimester Changes

New physical changes may appear as the uterus and baby grow.

👶 First Movements

Early movement may feel like fluttering, bubbles or gentle tapping. Timing varies between pregnancies.

🤰 Growing Belly

The abdomen expands, and clothes may begin to feel tighter.

🦴 Back, Hip or Side Aches

Changing posture and stretching tissues can cause mild discomfort.

🧴 Skin Changes

Stretch marks, darker patches or a darker line on the abdomen may develop.

🔥 Heartburn or Constipation

Digestive symptoms may continue as pregnancy progresses.

🦵 Leg Cramps

Calf or foot cramps can occur, particularly at night.

Ask about movement expectations

Her provider can explain when movement is usually noticed and when a change in movement should be assessed.

Second-Trimester Priorities

💙 How the partner can help in this stage

  • Join appointments or classes and learn about upcoming decisions together.
  • Help make daily movement and balanced meals easier.
  • Discuss finances, leave from work and practical support before the third trimester.
🌸

Third Trimester

The baby grows rapidly, appointments may become more frequent, and preparation for birth becomes a priority.

Common Third-Trimester Changes

The growing uterus can make normal activities, digestion and sleep more difficult.

😮‍💨 Mild Breathlessness

Normal activity may feel harder. Sudden or severe difficulty breathing is an urgent warning sign.

🚻 Frequent Urination

Pressure on the bladder may increase the need to urinate.

🛏️ Sleep Difficulty

Finding a comfortable position and sleeping continuously may become harder.

🔥 Heartburn

Pressure on the stomach may worsen indigestion or reflux.

🦶 Mild Ankle or Foot Swelling

Gradual mild swelling can occur. Sudden or extreme swelling, especially of the face or hands, needs urgent assessment.

〰️ Practice Tightenings

Irregular tightenings may occur. Regular, painful or increasingly frequent contractions should be discussed with the maternity team.

👜 Hospital Bag Checklist

Prepare this later in pregnancy according to the hospital's instructions and the maternity provider's advice.

For Mom

  • Identification and medical documents
  • Comfortable clothes and underwear
  • Nursing bra if planned
  • Toiletries and lip balm
  • Glasses or contact-lens supplies
  • Regular medicines approved by the hospital

For Baby

  • Going-home clothes
  • Blanket suitable for the weather
  • Diapers and wipes if the hospital requests them
  • Infant car seat where required for discharge

For the Partner

  • Phone and long charging cable
  • Change of clothes
  • Snacks and water where permitted
  • Cash, payment card and parking information
  • Important phone numbers

💙 How the partner can help before birth

  • Know the route, transport plan and hospital contact procedure.
  • Keep phones charged and essential documents together.
  • Learn her preferences while remaining flexible if medical needs change.
  • Know the urgent warning signs and speak up when something feels wrong.

Everyday Pregnancy Essentials

These apply throughout pregnancy unless her healthcare provider gives different instructions.

🥗 Food and Hydration

  • Choose vegetables, fruit, whole grains, protein foods and pasteurized dairy where suitable.
  • Use fully cooked eggs, meat, fish and shellfish.
  • Avoid alcohol and high-mercury fish.
  • Keep caffeine below the limit advised by her provider; ACOG uses less than 200 mg per day as moderate intake.
  • Drink fluids regularly, especially during nausea, hot weather or activity.

🚶 Movement and Rest

  • For an uncomplicated pregnancy, regular moderate activity is generally encouraged.
  • Walking, swimming and prenatal exercise may be suitable when approved.
  • Avoid activities with a high risk of falling, collision or overheating.
  • Stop and seek advice if exercise causes bleeding, severe pain, dizziness, chest pain or breathing difficulty.
  • Allow extra rest when fatigue is strong.

💊 Medicines and Supplements

  • Check before starting or stopping prescription or non-prescription medicine.
  • Discuss painkillers, cold medicines, vitamins, herbal products and traditional remedies.
  • Take prenatal vitamins exactly as advised.

🧠 Emotional Wellbeing

  • Talk openly about stress, fear and mood changes.
  • Ask for professional support when anxiety, sadness or distress becomes persistent or difficult to manage.
  • Thoughts of self-harm or harming the baby require immediate help.

🚨 Urgent Warning Signs

Seek urgent medical care or contact the maternity team immediately when a serious symptom occurs. Do not rely on this website to decide whether to wait.
  • Heavy vaginal bleeding, passing tissue or fluid leaking from the vagina
  • Severe abdominal, pelvic, shoulder or one-sided pain
  • Severe vomiting or inability to keep liquids down
  • A temperature of 38°C / 100.4°F or higher
  • Fainting or severe, persistent dizziness
  • A severe headache that will not go away or becomes worse
  • Blurred vision, flashing lights, blind spots or other vision changes
  • Chest pain, trouble breathing or a fast-beating heart
  • Sudden or extreme swelling of the face or hands
  • Later in pregnancy, the baby's movement stops or becomes noticeably slower
  • Thoughts of harming herself or the baby
  • A strong feeling that something is seriously wrong

Trust concerns

If a symptom feels severe, unusual or frightening, contact a qualified healthcare professional even when it is not listed above.
