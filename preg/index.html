🌱 First Trimester

Your wife is currently in this stage. Her body is going through major hormonal and physical changes.

Common First-Trimester Symptoms

These symptoms are commonly experienced during early pregnancy. Every pregnancy is different, so she may experience many, a few, or none of them.

😴 Tiredness

Feeling unusually tired or needing more sleep is very common, especially during the first 12 weeks.

🤢 Nausea or Vomiting

“Morning sickness” can happen at any time of day or night, not only in the morning.

💗 Tender Breasts

Breasts may feel sore, heavy, swollen or sensitive. Nipples may also become darker.

🚻 Frequent Urination

She may need to urinate more frequently, including during the night.

🎭 Mood Changes

Hormonal changes may cause emotional ups and downs, irritability, worry or crying more easily.

👃 Stronger Sense of Smell

Familiar smells may suddenly feel stronger or unpleasant. Food likes and dislikes may also change.

🎈 Bloating and Gas

Hormonal changes can slow digestion and cause bloating, gas, constipation or indigestion.

🔥 Heartburn

Indigestion or a burning feeling in the chest can happen, even during early pregnancy.

🩹 Mild Cramping

Mild cramps similar to period pains can occur as the body changes. Severe or one-sided pain needs medical advice.

💧 Vaginal Discharge

More clear or milky-white discharge can be normal when there is no strong smell, itching, soreness or irritation.

🤕 Mild Headaches or Dizziness

Hormonal and circulation changes can cause occasional headaches or lightheadedness.

✨ Few or No Symptoms

Some people have very mild symptoms or no obvious symptoms during early pregnancy.

💙 A reminder for both of you

Symptoms may be stronger on some days and milder on others. Pregnancy experiences are different for every person.

However, do not dismiss something that feels severe, unusual, persistent or worrying. Contact her healthcare provider whenever you are unsure.

Symptoms to Mention to Her Healthcare Provider

These do not always mean something is seriously wrong, but it is safer to ask for medical advice:

  • Any vaginal spotting or bleeding
  • Persistent abdominal or pelvic pain
  • Pain mainly on one side of the lower abdomen
  • Pain or burning while urinating
  • Discharge with a bad smell, itching or soreness
  • Frequent dizziness
  • Vomiting that makes eating or drinking difficult
  • A headache that is persistent or getting worse
  • Any symptom that feels unusual or worrying

Light spotting

Light spotting can happen in early pregnancy and does not automatically mean pregnancy loss. However, she should still inform her obstetrician, midwife or healthcare provider about bleeding during pregnancy.

First-Trimester Priorities

  • Arrange prenatal care and attend appointments.
  • Take the prenatal vitamin recommended by her provider.
  • Ask the provider about folic acid, iron and vitamin D.
  • Review all medicines and supplements with the provider.
  • Avoid alcohol, smoking and recreational drugs.
  • Eat safe, balanced meals and stay hydrated.
  • Allow extra time for rest and sleep.
  • Ask the provider which prenatal tests are recommended.

🌼 Second Trimester

Many people begin to feel more energetic, and the pregnancy becomes more physically noticeable.

Common Second-Trimester Changes

👶 Baby Movement

The first movements may feel like fluttering, bubbles or gentle tapping.

🤰 Growing Belly

The uterus and abdomen grow as the baby develops.

🦴 Back or Hip Discomfort

Changes in posture and body weight may cause mild aches.

🧴 Skin Changes

Stretch marks, darker skin patches or a dark line on the abdomen may appear.

🔥 Heartburn or Constipation

Digestive discomfort may continue as pregnancy progresses.

🦵 Leg Cramps

Some people experience calf or foot cramps, especially at night.

Second-Trimester Priorities

  • Attend all prenatal appointments.
  • Complete recommended scans and screening tests.
  • Continue appropriate physical activity.
  • Eat balanced meals and drink enough water.
  • Discuss baby movement expectations with the provider.
  • Ask about sleeping position and back-pain prevention.

🌸 Third Trimester

The baby grows rapidly, appointments become more frequent and preparation for birth begins.

Common Third-Trimester Changes

😮‍💨 Mild Shortness of Breath

The growing uterus may make normal activity feel more tiring. Sudden or severe breathing difficulty is urgent.

🚻 Frequent Urination

Pressure from the baby may increase the need to urinate.

🛏️ Sleep Difficulty

Finding a comfortable position may become more difficult.

🔥 Heartburn

Pressure on the stomach may make indigestion more noticeable.

🦶 Mild Foot or Ankle Swelling

Mild gradual swelling can occur. Sudden swelling of the face or hands needs prompt medical attention.

〰️ Practice Contractions

Irregular tightening may occur. Regular, painful or increasingly frequent contractions should be assessed.

Third-Trimester Priorities

  • Attend increasingly frequent prenatal appointments.
  • Learn what is normal for the baby’s movement.
  • Ask the provider when to call about reduced movement.
  • Learn the signs of labor and water breaking.
  • Prepare the hospital bag.
  • Prepare transport and emergency contact information.
  • Install the infant car seat when required.

✔ Daily Care Checklist

  • Take the prenatal vitamin recommended by her healthcare provider.
  • Drink water regularly throughout the day.
  • Eat fruits, vegetables, whole grains and protein.
  • Eat small meals if nausea or heartburn is a problem.
  • Rest whenever the body needs it.
  • Do gentle activity if approved by the healthcare provider.
  • Avoid alcohol, smoking and recreational drugs.
  • Note any new or concerning symptoms.

🥗 Pregnancy Nutrition

Good Food Choices

  • Green leafy vegetables
  • Broccoli, carrots and sweet potatoes
  • Fully cooked eggs
  • Pasteurized milk and yogurt
  • Pasteurized cheese
  • Fully cooked chicken and lean meat
  • Low-mercury fish
  • Beans, lentils and chickpeas
  • Brown rice, oats and whole grains
  • Fresh, thoroughly washed fruit
  • Nuts and seeds

Foods and Substances to Avoid

  • Alcohol
  • Smoking and secondhand smoke
  • Raw or undercooked fish
  • Raw or undercooked meat
  • Raw or partly cooked eggs where unsafe
  • Unpasteurized milk or cheese
  • Raw shellfish
  • High-mercury fish
  • More caffeine than her provider recommends
  • Unapproved herbal products or supplements

Spicy food

Spicy food is not usually harmful to the baby, but it may increase heartburn, nausea or indigestion for the mother.

🏃 Exercise and Movement

Common Pregnancy-Friendly Activities

  • Walking
  • Prenatal yoga
  • Swimming
  • Gentle stretching
  • Pelvic-floor exercises

Activities Usually Avoided

  • Contact sports
  • Scuba diving
  • Activities with a high risk of falling
  • Overheating
  • Exercises specifically restricted by her provider

Exercise safely

Her healthcare provider should confirm which activities are suitable, especially if the pregnancy has complications or activity restrictions.

💊 Medicines and Supplements

  • Check with a qualified healthcare provider before taking prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines or painkillers.
  • Take prenatal vitamins as recommended.
  • Discuss herbal teas, traditional remedies and supplements.
  • Do not stop an important prescribed medicine without medical advice.
  • Ask which vaccinations are recommended during pregnancy.

🚨 Get Urgent Medical Help

Contact her maternity provider, local emergency service or nearest hospital immediately when a serious symptom occurs.
  • Heavy vaginal bleeding or passing tissue
  • Severe abdominal, pelvic, shoulder or one-sided pain
  • Fainting or severe, persistent dizziness
  • Chest pain, difficulty breathing or a very fast heartbeat
  • A severe headache that does not go away or becomes worse
  • Blurred vision, flashing lights, blind spots or other vision changes
  • A temperature of 38°C / 100.4°F or higher
  • Severe vomiting or inability to keep liquids down
  • Signs of dehydration, such as very little urine or a very dry mouth
  • Sudden or extreme swelling of the face or hands
  • A gush or continuous leaking of fluid from the vagina
  • Regular painful contractions before the expected time
  • Later in pregnancy, the baby moves significantly less than usual
  • Thoughts of harming herself or the baby
  • Any strong feeling that something is seriously wrong
