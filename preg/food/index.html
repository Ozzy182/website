Pregnancy Guide Food Categories Limit or Avoid

Build balanced meals, not a “perfect” diet

Use this page as a practical idea list. Aim for a mix of vegetables and fruit, whole grains or other starchy foods, protein foods, calcium-rich foods and healthy fats.

Individual needs can differ because of allergies, diabetes, anemia, nausea, cultural diet, medicines or pregnancy complications. Her healthcare provider should give the final advice.

Four food-safety habits

  • Clean hands and produce
  • Separate raw and cooked foods
  • Cook foods thoroughly
  • Chill perishable foods promptly

Fruit & Vegetables

Choose different colors and wash produce thoroughly before eating or cutting.

Good choice

Leafy green vegetables

Useful in curries, soups, stir-fries, omelets and salads when washed carefully.

Examples: spinach, kale, broccoli, bok choy, mustard greens
Good choice

Orange vegetables

Roast, steam, boil or add to soups for an easy source of energy and nutrients.

Examples: sweet potato, carrot, pumpkin, squash
Good choice

Vitamin-C-rich produce

Pairs well with plant-based iron foods such as beans, lentils and leafy greens.

Examples: oranges, kiwi, guava, strawberries, bell peppers, tomatoes
Good choice

Easy everyday fruit

Fresh, frozen or canned fruit can be convenient. Choose options without lots of added sugar.

Examples: banana, apple, pear, berries, mango
Good choice

Avocado

Add to toast, rice bowls, wraps or salads for healthy fats and a creamy texture.

Serving ideas: avocado toast, guacamole, sliced avocado
Prepare safely

Raw and ready-to-eat produce

Wash whole produce under running water. Keep pre-cut fruit and packaged salads refrigerated and use before the use-by date.

Extra care: melon, bagged salad, fresh herbs, raw sprouts
🌾

Whole Grains & Starchy Foods

These foods provide energy and can be easier to tolerate when nausea is present.

Good choice

Oats and porridge

Top with fruit, yogurt, milk, nuts or seeds for a flexible breakfast.

Examples: oatmeal, overnight oats, oat porridge
Good choice

Rice and whole grains

Combine with vegetables, beans, eggs, meat or fish to build a balanced bowl.

Examples: brown rice, quinoa, barley, millet, buckwheat
Good choice

Bread, roti and pasta

Whole-grain choices add fiber, but familiar refined options may be easier during strong nausea.

Examples: whole-grain bread, roti, chapati, whole-wheat pasta
Good choice

Starchy vegetables

Bake, boil or mash and add a protein food and vegetables.

Examples: potato, sweet potato, corn, yam
🍳

Protein Foods

Use a variety of animal and plant proteins, prepared thoroughly and safely.

Good choice

Beans, lentils and soy foods

Affordable choices for soups, curries, salads, rice bowls and snacks.

Examples: lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans, tofu, tempeh, edamame
Prepare safely

Eggs

Cook eggs until the white and yolk are firm, or use pasteurized eggs for recipes that remain raw or lightly cooked.

Ideas: hard-boiled eggs, fully cooked omelet, scrambled eggs
Prepare safely

Chicken and turkey

Cook poultry thoroughly and keep raw juices away from ready-to-eat foods.

Ideas: chicken soup, roasted chicken, turkey meatballs
Prepare safely

Lean red meat

Can provide protein and iron. Avoid raw or undercooked meat.

Examples: lean beef, pork, lamb
Good choice

Nuts, seeds and nut butters

Convenient additions to toast, oatmeal, yogurt and fruit when there is no allergy.

Examples: peanuts, almonds, walnuts, chia, sesame, peanut butter
Prepare safely

Deli meats and hot dogs

Safer when reheated until steaming hot. Keep chilled and avoid eating past the use-by date.

Examples: sliced turkey, ham, hot dogs, cooked sausage
🥛

Dairy & Calcium-Rich Foods

Choose pasteurized products, or use fortified alternatives when dairy is unsuitable.

Good choice

Pasteurized milk

Use on its own, in porridge, smoothies or cooking.

Options: dairy milk, lactose-free milk
Good choice

Pasteurized yogurt

A convenient snack with fruit, oats, nuts or seeds.

Examples: plain yogurt, Greek yogurt, kefir made from pasteurized milk
Good choice

Pasteurized cheeses

Check the label. Hard cheeses and pasteurized options are generally simpler choices.

Examples: cheddar, mozzarella, cottage cheese, cream cheese
Good choice

Fortified plant alternatives

Choose unsweetened products fortified with calcium and vitamin D when possible.

Examples: fortified soy milk, oat milk, pea-protein milk
Check carefully

Soft and blue cheeses

Rules vary by product and country. Check whether the cheese is pasteurized and follow local pregnancy guidance; cooking until steaming hot can reduce risk for some products.

Examples: brie, camembert, blue cheese, queso fresco
🐟

Fish & Seafood

Choose lower-mercury fish and cook seafood thoroughly.

Good choice

Lower-mercury oily fish

FDA and ACOG guidance supports 2–3 servings of lower-mercury fish per week during pregnancy.

Examples: salmon, sardines, trout, anchovies, herring
Good choice

Other lower-mercury seafood

Cook until opaque and steaming hot. Frozen and canned options can be convenient.

Examples: shrimp, pollock, cod, tilapia, catfish
Check serving limits

Tuna

Mercury levels vary by type. Canned light tuna is generally lower than albacore or bigeye tuna; follow the current FDA chart or local guidance.

Check label: light tuna, albacore/white tuna, bigeye tuna
Avoid raw

Raw or undercooked seafood

Avoid raw fish and shellfish during pregnancy. Cold-smoked or cured fish may also require special precautions depending on local guidance.

Examples: sashimi, raw sushi, oysters, ceviche
High mercury

Fish to avoid

Avoid fish listed as highest in mercury by local authorities.

FDA examples: shark, swordfish, king mackerel, marlin, orange roughy, tilefish, bigeye tuna

Simple Snacks & Nausea-Friendly Ideas

Small, frequent snacks may be easier than large meals, especially in early pregnancy.

Simple option

Plain dry foods

Keep a small portion nearby for mornings or periods of nausea.

Examples: crackers, toast, rice cakes, dry cereal
Balanced snack

Fruit with yogurt

Combines carbohydrate, protein and calcium in a small portion.

Examples: banana and yogurt, berries and Greek yogurt
Balanced snack

Fruit or toast with nut butter

A portable option that may keep hunger away longer than fruit alone.

Examples: apple and peanut butter, toast and almond butter
Balanced snack

Hummus with vegetables or pita

Use refrigerated hummus before its use-by date and keep it chilled.

Examples: carrot sticks, cucumber, bell pepper, whole-grain pita
Check ingredients

Ginger foods or drinks

Some people find ginger helpful for nausea. Check sugar, caffeine and supplement-strength products with the healthcare provider.

Examples: ginger tea, ginger biscuits, fresh ginger in meals
💧

Drinks

Regular fluids are important, especially with vomiting, heat or activity.

Best everyday choice

Water

Sip regularly. Chilled water, sparkling water or water with lemon may be easier during nausea.

Ideas: plain water, lemon water, unsweetened sparkling water
Good choice

Milk and homemade smoothies

Use pasteurized milk or fortified alternatives, washed produce and clean equipment.

Ideas: milk, yogurt smoothie, fortified soy drink
Limit caffeine

Coffee, tea and caffeinated drinks

ACOG advises keeping caffeine below 200 mg per day from all sources. Check serving size because caffeine varies widely.

Sources: coffee, tea, cola, chocolate, energy drinks
Check first

Herbal teas and supplements

“Natural” does not automatically mean pregnancy-safe. Ask the healthcare provider about specific herbs and concentrated products.

Check: herbal blends, powders, tonics, traditional remedies
Avoid

Alcohol

Avoid alcohol during pregnancy. Choose alcohol-free alternatives, but check labels because some products may still contain small amounts.

Examples: wine, beer, spirits, cocktails

⚠️ Foods to Avoid or Handle Carefully

Pregnancy increases the risk of serious illness from some foodborne germs. These reminders are intentionally cautious and should be adjusted to local medical guidance.

Avoid

  • Alcohol
  • Raw or undercooked meat, poultry, fish, shellfish and eggs
  • Unpasteurized milk and foods made from it
  • Fish identified locally as highest in mercury
  • Raw sprouts unless thoroughly cooked
  • Liver and liver products unless her provider specifically advises otherwise

Handle carefully

  • Wash fruit, vegetables and herbs thoroughly
  • Reheat deli meats and hot dogs until steaming hot
  • Keep refrigerated foods cold and respect use-by dates
  • Check soft cheeses, smoked fish and prepared salads against local guidance
  • Keep total caffeine below the limit advised by her provider
  • Check local advisories for fish caught by family or friends
